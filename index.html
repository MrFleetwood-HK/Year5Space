🗺️ Unit Overview & Science Core

Learning Journey
Lesson 1: Earth, Sun, and Moon

How do these three massive celestial bodies interact? Is Earth really as small as a grain of sand compared to the Sun?

Lesson 2: Solar System Model

Building a true-to-scale model of our cosmic neighborhood. Why are the inner planets so close together while outer planets are millions of miles apart?

Lesson 3: Planet Research

Investigating extreme worlds! Which planet has storms bigger than planet Earth itself?

Lesson 4: Phases of the Moon

Why does the Moon change shape in the night sky? Does the Moon actually glow, or is it playing tricks with sunlight?

Lesson 5: Night & Day

Uncovering how Earth's spinning motion creates sunrise and sunset. While we sleep in Hong Kong, who is walking into school?

Lesson 6: The Seasons

Exploring Earth's secret 23.5-degree tilt. Why does hot summer in London happen at the exact same time as freezing winter in Sydney?

Lesson 7: The Future

Mars colonies, lunar bases, and interstellar rockets. How will humans live on other planets in your lifetime?

Lesson 8: Earth & Space Assessment

Final end-of-unit assessment to see if we could become astronauts!

💡 Mass vs. Weight Vocabulary:
• Mass (kg): The amount of "stuff" (matter) inside an object. Mass never changes anywhere in space!
• Weight (N - Newtons): The downward gravitational pull on an object's mass ($W = \text{mass} \cdot g$). Massive Jupiter pulls down with 24.79 N/kg, making weight much higher!

☀️ Space Environment & History

🌍 Atmosphere Layers & Altitude Breakdown

🌌 Exosphere (Deep Space Boundary) 600+ km
🛰️ Thermosphere (Space Stations & Auroras) 400 km
☄️ Mesosphere (Meteor Disintegration) 50 - 85 km
✈️ Stratosphere (Ozone Layer & Jet Streams) 12 - 50 km
🌧️ Troposphere (Weather & All Earth Life) 0 - 12 km
Troposphere: Lowest layer where all weather happens, clouds form, and commercial airplanes fly.
Stratosphere: Contains the vital ozone layer that absorbs harmful UV radiation from the Sun.
Mesosphere: Coldest layer where meteors burn up upon entry as "shooting stars."
Thermosphere: Extremely high-temperature upper atmosphere where ISS orbits and Northern Lights glow.
Exosphere: The final frontier boundary where Earth's atmosphere gradually fades into deep space.

🌪️ Solar Wind Speed: ~420 km/s

Cosmic Sprinkler: The Sun continuously sprays charged particles across space at over 400 km/s! When these solar particles collide with Earth's magnetic shield, they glow as the Northern Lights!

⏱️ Earth Sunlight Travel Time: 8m 20s

Sunlight travels 150 Million km across empty space at 300,000 km/s to reach Earth. Light hitting your eyes right now left the Sun over 8 minutes ago!

🔴 Mars Distance: Sunlight travels 228 Million km to Mars, taking ~12m 40s!

💡 Did You Know?

A rocket is the launch vehicle that pushes things into space, a satellite is an object that orbits a planet, and a spaceship (or spacecraft) is a broader term for any vehicle designed to travel or operate in outer space, often carrying people or cargo.

🚀 Rockets
  • What they do: Carry payloads, satellites, and spaceships out of Earth's atmosphere.
  • How they move: Use massive chemical engines and fuel to blast upward against gravity.
  • Lifespan: Active only during launch, and pieces are usually discarded or burned up.
🛰️ Satellites
  • What they do: Handle communication, weather tracking, and GPS navigation.
  • How they move: Orbit planets because their forward speed balances gravity.
  • Lifespan: Designed to stay in space and work for many years.
🛸 Spaceships
  • What they do: Transport equipment or human crews through space.
  • How they move: Navigate between orbits, planets, or deep space destinations.
  • Lifespan: Built for long-term missions and can often return to Earth or dock at stations.

📜 Famous Successful Rockets

1957 — Sputnik 1 (R-7 Rocket) First artificial satellite successfully launched into Earth orbit.
1961 — Vostok 1 (Vostok Rocket) Carrying Yuri Gagarin, making him the first human in outer space.
1969 — Apollo 11 (Saturn V) The most powerful rocket ever flown, carrying astronauts to walk on the Moon.
1981 — Space Shuttle (STS-1) First reusable winged orbital spacecraft, taking off like a rocket and landing like a plane.
2020 — Falcon 9 (Crew Dragon) First commercial reusable booster rocket to re-fly human astronauts to the ISS.
